# Structure: one idea, one shape

Contents: 1 Why "nice shots in a row" is not editing · 2 Finding the one idea · 3 Twelve proven structures · 4 Openings and endings · 5 Breath inserts · 6 Choosing with the footage you have

## 1. Why "nice shots in a row" is not editing

A sequence of beautiful shots with hard cuts between them is a slideshow, even with polished transitions. Every strong short film has a **rule the viewer learns in about 2 seconds**; the rule repeats, then at the end it either completes or breaks. If you cannot write that rule in one sentence, there is no film yet, only material.

Test: shuffle the shots. If the meaning does not change, the cut has no structure.

## 2. Finding the one idea

1. Which rule will the viewer learn in the first 2 s (48–50 frames)? Process→result, same frame/new thing, two halves compared, one colour, one object that travels, one gesture repeated.
2. Does the footage support it? Locked-frame and split-screen structures need shots that were filmed for them; you cannot rescue them later. Check the scan strips.
3. What does the rule do at the end? It completes (the plate is finished, the room is full), it breaks (the last round lasts twice as long, nothing enters), or it merges into one hero.
4. Write the sentence into `edit_plan.json` → `"idea"`. Every shot's `"reason"` must serve it.

The film's subject stays the subject: a burger film tells the burger; place and atmosphere decorate it and never replace it. The film **ends on the subject's result**: the serving, the first bite, the finished room, the worn garment. It does not end on a random atmosphere detail.

## 3. Twelve proven structures

Durations are at 24/25 fps for a 10–20 s film (240–500 frames). The rule must be learned within 48 frames; the last 2–3 s complete or break it. Shared numbers (shortest shot, dissolves, push) are in `numbers.md`.

Structures marked **[only if shot for it]** (2, 3, 9, 11) look like a template in an agent's hands unless the footage was filmed for that exact structure (matched framing, locked camera, planned poses). If it was not, do not build them in the edit; pick another structure.

1. **Locked frame, the subject changes.** Camera and light fixed; each round only the object or person changes; text, if any, stays in one place. Beat: empty frame 0.7 s → object enters (12 f) → holds ~2 s → leaves; 5–6 rounds; the last round twice as long or nothing enters. Needs a tripod, a floor mark and equal light.
2. **Process / result split [only if shot for it].** Top half preparation, bottom half the finished thing, at the same time. 3 pairs × ~1.4 s joined by hard cuts (no flash, no wipe), then 2 s of the hero full frame. The viewer builds the link without a "jump" from raw to cooked. Needs pairs filmed with matching framing and camera height.
3. **Two places or two times split [only if shot for it].** Dining room / kitchen, noon / night, made / enjoyed. Both halves same framing and camera height; 3–4 pairs × 1–1.3 s; at the end the halves merge. A split needs a sentence ("we make / you enjoy"); a meaningless split is removed.
4. **Breathing wide.** The same locked wide shot of the place returns between inserts: wide 60 f, insert 48 f, wide 48 f, insert 48 f… 5–6 parts. The place "breathes".
5. **Object continuity.** One object (a candlestick, a bell, a plate pattern, a logo found in the world) appears in every shot; cuts land when the object passes the centre; the last shot holds the logo 72 f.
6. **Match cuts.** Every cut carries shape, colour or movement direction (round plate → sun → glass rim; pour of butter → pour of golden sauce). 5 shots × 48–55 f; the movement starts 3–4 frames before the cut. These are planned, not found: check the strips for matching shapes.
7. **Foreground reveal.** A foreground element (glass, leaf, door, hand) slides and opens the main shot; the cut is hidden on the occluder. Occluder fills the frame 12 f → cut → new shot 60 f.
8. **Reflection / water.** The second shot is the reflection of the first, or inside the water; water is the transition. Descend to the surface 36 f, reflection 24 f, new world 48 f.
9. **One gesture, no cut.** A lid lifts, a plate builds from above: one fluid gesture of 120–150 f in one locked take, then the full result 48 f. The stop-motion variant (a table that sets itself in poses of 10–14 f) is **[only if shot for it]**: locked camera, one pose per object move, planned on set.
10. **Fixed title, changing shots.** A small serif title sits in one place for the whole film; 6–8 shots of 45–55 f change beneath it; the title never moves; it dissolves out over another shot. Frame shots with clean space for the title.
11. **Colour as one move [only if shot for it].** Black-and-white opens (kitchen, hands, people); colour opens on the subject's moment (34 f), inside the action; at most once or twice in a film. Use it only when the brief or the shoot was designed around it; as a found effect it is a cliché.
12. **The arc (default skeleton).** Cause to result, one direction: 0–2 s place and light; 2–6 s craft (knife, fire, hands; 2 shots × 2 s); 6–11 s the serve or the key action in one held shot; 11–15 s the rule completes or breaks (hero 3 s); 15–17 s logo on black or the fixed title. The arc rides on one of structures 1–11; the structure carries the arc.

**Speed and register.** Premium places want shots of 2–3 s, few cuts and movement *inside* the frame. Fast montage (< 1 s shots) reads as energy and youth; use it only when the brief asks for it, and even then a shot must last long enough to be understood. A run of fast cuts ends in a held release shot. Minimum lengths and the register table: `numbers.md` §2–3.

## 4. Openings and endings

**Opening.** Frame 0 is already a strong image: motion, texture or surprise. No black, no logo card first, no flash. The rule is visible in the first shot. In social formats the subject or product appears within 2 s, at the latest by 5 s. Text normally lives only on the end card; when the brief asks for an opening logo, a brand film may put a small logo over a wide, calm opening shot for 1.4–2.6 s with a simple opacity rise, then no more text.

**Ending.** Choose one: (a) one hero shot of the result, 3–4 s; (b) the rule breaks: the last round runs long; (c) a slow fall to black on the result; (d) a small, fixed logo. The end card lands on the musical resolution, not an abrupt stop, and holds 1.5–2.5 s. Never freeze the last frame and never end on a filler detail.

If the film should loop, the last shot's composition and motion direction match the first.

## 5. Breath inserts (place and light details)

Short details of the place (light on a wall, water, a curtain moving, a terrace) used as **breaths between main shots**: about 12 frames each, 2–3 between two main shots, then back to the subject immediately. They belong to food and restaurant films; in hotel and architecture films prefer shots of 1 s or more (`numbers.md` §2). They must read as part of the same film (same light, same palette). Keep about 60% or more of the running time on the main subject. They never end the film.

## 6. Choosing with the footage you have

- Look at all scan strips first. Group shots by what they can *do*: locked frames (static camera), moves, gestures at a peak, textures, wides of the place, people.
- Many locked frames of different objects at one spot → structure 1 or 10.
- Raw and finished versions of the same thing → structure 2.
- Two spaces or times filmed with similar framing → structure 3.
- One good wide + many details → structure 4.
- A recurring object or colour → 5 or 6.
- Strong high-frame-rate gestures → build the arc (12) around one slow-motion hero moment.
- Use every good moment the footage gives; the length follows the good footage, never padding. Do not leave a strong shot out because of a length target; propose the longer cut and say what you would trim.
- No two shots with the same person/face/look in one short film unless the structure repeats them on purpose; variety makes a place feel full.
